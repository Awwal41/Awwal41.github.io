About

Wells lose production when liquid loads the tubing or sand comes up with the flow. From the University of Lagos through later collaborations, my papers use field data and models to see liquid loading, sand, and hydrate temperature early enough to act, and to weigh oil recovery together with carbon storage. A related study improves biodiesel yield from waste fat by pairing experiment with computation.

At Michigan the bottleneck moved from the well to the simulation: the physics is trustworthy, and the setup is slow. I build public agents and research codes, and private tools for documents and for running models on a laptop, a cluster, or a phone. Private work is described at the level of the contribution, and the repository stays closed.

2026 Research

TurboChIMES

Accurate atomistic models often cost too much to use while a material is still being designed. TurboChIMES, my first-author preprint, stacks machine-learned interatomic models so the simulation stays faithful and becomes fast enough to search with. A designer can try more chemistries before a long campaign. This is the story the audience chose as People’s Choice at Michigan’s Three Minute Thesis.

2026 Research

Hierarchical transfer learning

A new chemistry usually means fitting an interatomic model all over again, and that fit is the slow, expensive part. With Rebecca Lindsey and colleagues at Lawrence Livermore, I am second author on a paper in npj Computational Materials that carries what a model has already learned into the next system. Labs can reach a usable potential with less data and less compute.

2026 Research

Alchemical compression

Every added element multiplies the training burden of a machine-learned potential, so coverage stays narrow. With T. Sundberg and Rebecca Lindsey I am compressing that elemental space so one potential can span more of the periodic table. Materials design can then reach a wider set of elements. The direction is public as a 2026 AIChE contribution.

2025 Software

MaterialSim

A molecular-dynamics study still begins as a specialist’s input deck. MaterialSim is a public agent that turns one sentence into the simulation, computes properties such as structure and transport, and explains what came back. The engine runs locally, so a researcher gets a real run and an interpretation in the same conversation.

2026 Software Private

EZRA

Open language models are free, and getting one installed, pointed at your papers, and onto a cluster is still its own project. EZRA downloads a model, runs an agent on the machine in front of you, prepares documents for that model, and stages the same job on SLURM. A materials or chemistry researcher can work locally the same day and reuse the workflow on the cluster.

2025 Research

Droplet-film model for liquid loading

When gas in a well slows down, liquid falls back, pressure rises, and production can stop. I built a physics-informed model of the droplet film behind that failure, with public documentation, extending the liquid-loading study I published with Kingsley Abhulimen. The aim is to see the loading early enough to act before a shutdown.

2025 Research

Sand production

Sand produced with oil or gas wears out equipment and cuts a well’s output, and the warning often arrives after the damage. My first-author paper in Petroleum Science and Engineering uses field data to predict and manage that sand, on top of public notebooks for reservoir rock typing. Operators get a clearer view of which wells need attention.

2025 Product Private

Edna

A question about a long technical archive is only useful when the answer can be traced to a page. Edna is a private assistant with a desktop app and a web client: you choose the folders, ask, and the reply points at the passage it used. The archive stays searchable, and the source stays attached, on one computer or on a service you run.

2023– Software

ChIMES toolchain

A machine-learned interatomic model helps only after it can be fit, checked, and called from the simulator a lab already trusts. I work in the public ChIMES stack that does the active learning, the least-squares fit, and the handoff into outside codes. The model can leave the notebook and run inside an existing simulation.
